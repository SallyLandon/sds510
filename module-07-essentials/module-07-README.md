# Module 07 — YouTube GLP-1 vs. Weight-Loss Journey Comment Analysis
# Research Questions
This project examines how online discussions differ between medication-based and lifestyle-based approaches to weight loss. Guided by GTR, these research questions were designed to be **narrow enough for empirical testing**, yet meaningful for broader theory development.

### **RQ1 — Responsibility Framing**
Do GLP-1–related comments contain more responsibility-oriented language (“I should,” “I need to”) than comments from weight-loss journey videos?  
*GTR prediction:* Medicalization may shift responsibility from moral effort to clinical obligation.

### **RQ2 — Future Orientation**
Is future-oriented language (“in the future,” “long-term,” “years from now”) more prevalent in GLP-1 discussions?  
*GTR prediction:* Medical contexts may expand users’ temporal horizons.

### **RQ3 — Agency Framing**
Do communities differ in how they express agency (“I can,” “I can’t”), and do these phrases carry different meanings across groups?  
*GTR prediction:* Agency may shift from self-control to physiological constraint.

### **RQ4 — Cognitive Scarcity / Overwhelm**
Is scarcity or overwhelm (“overwhelmed,” “too much,” “I can’t handle”) more common in journey comments?  
*GTR prediction:* Lifestyle modification entails a higher cognitive load than medication reliance.

### **RQ5 — KWIC Discursive Differences**
How do commenters *use* key phrases in context, and do KWIC patterns reveal deeper narrative differences?  
*GTR prediction:* Identical phrases may express different psychological constructs depending on the community.

# Scholarly Framing 

Although not a full literature review, this project draws on three foundational scholarly sources to contextualize the analysis and identify heuristic “unknowns” worth exploring.

### **1. Temporal Discounting & Preventive Behavior — Ainslie (2012)**
Ainslie’s work on intertemporal choice explains why individuals often struggle with long-term preventive behavior.  
*Relevance:* Supports GTR’s emphasis on future orientation; medicalization (e.g., GLP-1s) may shift temporal trade-offs.

### **2. Social Cognitive Theory and Human Agency — Bandura & Cervone (2023)**
Bandura’s Social Cognitive Theory (SCT) posits that human behavior is shaped by a dynamic interaction of personal beliefs, social context, and environmental constraints. Central to SCT is the concept of **perceived self-efficacy**, which determines whether individuals believe they can influence outcomes.  
*Relevance:* Provides the theoretical backbone for GTR’s **agency dimension**. SCT predicts that perceived agency is contextual, malleable, and domain-specific — precisely the distinction observed between GLP-1 comments (agency framed as physiological or medication-mediated) and journey comments (agency framed as willpower and self-discipline).

### **3. Cognitive Scarcity — Mullainathan & Shafir (2013)**
Scarcity of attention and bandwidth impairs self-regulation and health-related decision-making.  
*Relevance:* Predicts higher cognitive burden in lifestyle-change communities, consistent with journey KWICs.


Together, these sources provide theoretical grounding for why GLP-1 and journey discourse might differ.

# Initial Findings (“Hunches”)

Before analyzing the data, my original GTR-based expectation was that *journey* videos would show higher levels of agency, responsibility, future-oriented thinking, and cognitive scarcity, because lifestyle change traditionally relies on self-discipline, long-term planning, and personal effort.

However, the data revealed a different pattern:

- **GLP-1 discourse shows significantly higher responsibility and future-oriented language**, reflecting a shift from moral effort (“I should try harder”) to clinical framing (“my doctor says I should…”).
- **Journey discourse shows stronger expressions of scarcity and emotional burden**, consistent with the cognitive effort required for lifestyle change.
- **Keywords carry different meanings across communities**, supporting GTR’s prediction that agency and responsibility are domain-specific and context-dependent.
- **Medicalization appears to extend temporal horizons**, suggesting a mechanism behind the “erosion of prevention.”

These unexpected findings refine the GTR framework and justify expanding the contextural analysis in the Proficient Badge.
