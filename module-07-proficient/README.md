This project brings together two methodological approaches I never expected to integrate: a YouTube discourse analysis and a Structural Equation Model (SEM) using MIDUS psychosocial data. The idea for this mixed-method design emerged from Dr. Halavais's earlier feedback in Module 7 Essentials, which encouraged me to look beyond surface-level predictors and toward the deeper psychological structures shaping preventive health behavior.

The YouTube component examines how people negotiate responsibility, agency, and long-term effort when discussing weight loss in public online spaces. Although YouTube comments are not representative of any population, they provide a naturalistic lens into how people construct meaning, assign blame, and express emotional strain or resilience in real time.

The quantitative component extends my earlier work using machine learning to identify predictors of preventive health behavior. Based on Dr. Halavais's suggestion at the start of the semester, I incorporated a Structural Equation Model (SEM) to formally test the pathways connecting locus of control, emotional capacity, social support, and future-oriented health concern to Health Effort Behavior (HEB). This has been one of the most rewarding expansions of my analysis this term.

The final project is on the longer side, but the topic has become something of a personal research direction—a tentative attempt at a “unifying framework” for understanding why individuals differ so dramatically in sustained behavior change. I refer to this developing structure as Generalized Temporal Responsibility (GTR).

I also want to note transparently that I used AI tools (ChatGPT and Google Gemini) throughout the process. Their support included proofreading, restructuring sections, clarifying theoretical connections, and improving academic tone. All ideas, modeling decisions, and interpretations remain my own.
